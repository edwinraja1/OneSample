💡 Lesson 1: Central Limit Theorem

Watch the sampling distribution of the estimator (Panel 3) grow. As you raise sample size (\(n\)), the sampling distribution converges to a symmetrical, normal shape regardless of the population parameters.

📉 Lesson 2: Standard Error (SE)

The standard deviation of the sampling distribution is the Standard Error: $$SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$ Notice how the spread of Panel 3 dramatically shrinks when you raise sample size (\(n\)). This represents higher estimation precision.

🎯 Lesson 3: Standardization & Z/T Scale

Look at the bottom scale in Panel 3. It represents the standard normal scale ($Z$-scores). The shaded green zone between \(\pm 1.96\) defines the acceptance region under \(\alpha=0.05\). The red zones on the sides are rejection regions.

🔬 Lesson 4: Two-Sample Hypothesis Test

In Panel 4, draw a comparison group. We calculate its mean (\(\bar{x}_B\)) and compute a \(t\)-statistic against the null mean (\(\mu_0\)): $$t = \frac{\bar{x}_B - \mu_0}{s_B / \sqrt{n_B}}$$ The resulting \(t\)-value is mapped onto the standard scale as a big red pulsating dot. If it lands in the red zones, we reject the null hypothesis!
